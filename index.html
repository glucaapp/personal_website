Scientific interests & goals

How do neutron stars reveal themselves?

I investigate how accreting binary systems link X-ray phenomenology, binary evolution, and the continuous gravitational-wave signals they may produce.

At the Max Planck Institute for Gravitational Physics in Hannover, I work on continuous gravitational-wave sources: rapidly spinning neutron stars whose tiny asymmetries may radiate very weak gravitational radiation over very long timespans.

Accreting neutron stars in binary star systems are especially compelling candidates. As matter falls from an accretion disc, it produces X-rays and transfers angular momentum to the neutron star, increasing its spin rate. I model these systems to make gravitational-wave surveys more sensitive and to understand the Galactic population they belong to.
